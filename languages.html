Numbers & time

Numbers are built by combining: 十一じゅういち is 10 + 1 = 11, and 二十三にじゅうさん is 2 × 10 + 3 = 23. Hours are the number plus 時じ (ji), minutes the number plus 分ふん (fun or pun), and half past is 半はん (han). Add に (ni) for “at” (三時さんじに = at three) and ごろ (goro) for “around” (三時さんじごろ).

Basics

Who: I, you, he…

Japanese drops the pronoun whenever the context makes it clear. To address someone, people often use their name plus さん (san) instead of あなた.

EnglishJapaneseRomajiNote
I私わたしwatashi
youあなたanataOften replaced by the person's name + さん (san); anata can sound too direct.
he彼かれkare
she彼女かのじょkanojoAlso means “girlfriend”.
we私たちわたしたちwatashitachi
you (plural)あなたたちanatatachi
they彼らかれらkareraWomen only: 彼女かのじょたち (kanojotachi).

The verb does not change with the person

話はなします (hanashimasu) can mean I speak, you speak, he speaks, we speak… The context, or a pronoun that is usually left out, tells who. The verb only changes for politeness and tense (話はなします, 話はなしません = does not speak, 話はなしました = spoke). “My family” is 私わたしの家族かぞく and “your family” is あなたの家族かぞく (の marks possession), but when it is obvious you just say 家族かぞく; for someone else’s family you add ご: ご家族かぞく.

JapaneseRomajiMeaning
私わたしは週末しゅうまつに家族かぞくと話はなします。Watashi wa shūmatsu ni kazoku to hanashimasu.I talk with my family on weekends.
あなたは週末しゅうまつにご家族かぞくと話はなしますか。Anata wa shūmatsu ni gokazoku to hanashimasu ka.Do you talk with your family on weekends?

No plural

Japanese nouns have no plural: 友達ともだち (tomodachi) is one friend or several. The context tells, or you count: 友達ともだちが三人さんにん (tomodachi ga san-nin) = three friends. For people, たち (達たち) can mark the plural, as in 私わたしたち (watashitachi) = we.

Questions and answers

Add か (ka) at the end of a sentence to make it a question. Answer はい (hai) for yes and いいえ (iie) for no. With a verb you repeat the verb: 飲のみます (nomimasu) or 飲のみません (nomimasen). With a です sentence you can say はい、そうです (hai, sō desu: yes, that’s right) and, for no, いいえ、〜ではありません (iie, … dewa arimasen) or just いいえ、違ちがいます (iie, chigaimasu: no, that’s not it). Careful with i-adjectives: their negative is not ではありません but くないです, as in 新あたらしくないです (atarashikunai desu). The plain, casual forms are ではない (dewa nai) or じゃない (ja nai).

JapaneseRomajiMeaning
飲のみますか。Nomimasu ka?Do you drink?
はい、飲のみます。Hai, nomimasu.Yes, I drink.
いいえ、飲のみません。Iie, nomimasen.No, I do not drink.
水みずですか。Mizu desu ka?Is it water?
はい、そうです。Hai, sō desu.Yes, it is (that’s right).
いいえ、水みずではありません。Iie, mizu dewa arimasen.No, it is not water.
いいえ、違ちがいます。Iie, chigaimasu.No, that’s not it.
新あたらしいですか。Atarashii desu ka?Is it new?
いいえ、新あたらしくないです。Iie, atarashikunai desu.No, it is not new.

Family words: mine and yours

Japanese uses one word for your own relatives and another, more polite, for other people’s. 姉あね (ane) is simply “older sister” and 兄あに (ani) “older brother”: Japanese always says whether a sibling is older (姉あね, 兄あに) or younger (妹いもうと imōto, 弟おとうと otōto).

Little words (particles)

Particles come after the word they mark. は is read “wa”, を “o” and へ “e”.

ParticleWhat it doesExample
は (wa)marks the topic私わたしは (watashi wa) = as for me
を (o)marks the objectお茶ちゃを飲のみます (ocha o nomimasu) = I drink tea
に (ni)time, “at”週末しゅうまつに (shūmatsu ni) = on the weekend
へ (e)direction, “to”空港くうこうへ (kūkō e) = to the airport
で (de)means, “by”地下鉄ちかてつで (chikatetsu de) = by subway
と (to)“with”友達ともだちと (tomodachi to) = with a friend
の (no)possession私わたしの家族かぞく (watashi no kazoku) = my family
か (ka)makes a question飲のみますか (nomimasu ka) = do you drink?
が (ga)marks what is liked or wanted (and often the subject)バスケットボールが好すきです (basukettobōru ga suki desu) = I like basketball
も (mo)“also”私わたしも (watashi mo) = me too
