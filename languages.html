Numbers & time

Numbers are built by combining: 十一 is 10 + 1 = 11, and 二十三 is 2 × 10 + 3 = 23. Hours are the number plus 時 (ji), minutes the number plus 分 (fun or pun), and half past is 半 (han). Add に (ni) for “at” (三時に = at three) and ごろ (goro) for “around” (三時ごろ).

Basics

Who: I, you, he…

Japanese drops the pronoun whenever the context makes it clear. To address someone, people often use their name plus さん (san) instead of あなた.

EnglishJapaneseRomajiNote
I私わたしwatashi
youあなたanataOften replaced by the person's name + さん (san); anata can sound too direct.
he彼かれkare
she彼女かのじょkanojoAlso means “girlfriend”.
we私たちわたしたちwatashitachi
you (plural)あなたたちanatatachi
they彼らかれらkareraWomen only: 彼女たち (kanojotachi).

The verb does not change with the person

話します (hanashimasu) can mean I speak, you speak, he speaks, we speak… The context, or a pronoun that is usually left out, tells who. The verb only changes for politeness and tense (話します, 話しません = does not speak, 話しました = spoke). “My family” is 私の家族 and “your family” is あなたの家族 (の marks possession), but when it is obvious you just say 家族; for someone else’s family you add ご: ご家族.

JapaneseRomajiMeaning
私は週末に家族と話します。Watashi wa shūmatsu ni kazoku to hanashimasu.I talk with my family on weekends.
あなたは週末にご家族と話しますか。Anata wa shūmatsu ni gokazoku to hanashimasu ka.Do you talk with your family on weekends?

No plural

Japanese nouns have no plural: 友達 (tomodachi) is one friend or several. The context tells, or you count: 友達が三人 (tomodachi ga san-nin) = three friends. For people, たち (達) can mark the plural, as in 私たち (watashitachi) = we.

Questions and answers

Add か (ka) at the end of a sentence to make it a question. Answer はい (hai) for yes and いいえ (iie) for no.

JapaneseRomajiMeaning
飲みますか。Nomimasu ka?Do you drink?
はい、飲みます。Hai, nomimasu.Yes, I drink.
いいえ、飲みません。Iie, nomimasen.No, I do not drink.

Family words: mine and yours

Japanese uses one word for your own relatives and another, more polite, for other people’s. 姉 (ane) is simply “older sister” and 兄 (ani) “older brother”: Japanese always says whether a sibling is older (姉, 兄) or younger (妹 imōto, 弟 otōto).

Little words (particles)

Particles come after the word they mark. は is read “wa”, を “o” and へ “e”.

ParticleWhat it doesExample
は (wa)marks the topic私は (watashi wa) = as for me
を (o)marks the objectお茶を飲みます (ocha o nomimasu) = I drink tea
に (ni)time, “at”週末に (shūmatsu ni) = on the weekend
へ (e)direction, “to”空港へ (kūkō e) = to the airport
で (de)means, “by”地下鉄で (chikatetsu de) = by subway
と (to)“with”友達と (tomodachi to) = with a friend
の (no)possession私の家族 (watashi no kazoku) = my family
か (ka)makes a question飲みますか (nomimasu ka) = do you drink?
も (mo)“also”私も (watashi mo) = me too
