Breast cancer survival

Predicting 8-year survival from gene expression.

← All projects

  • R
  • glmnet
  • Random forest
  • limma
  • Genomics

The question

Can we tell, from clinical and gene-expression data, which breast cancer patients will survive more than 96 months (8 years)? Long-term predictions are hard because time plays a big role, and the theoretical best on the held-out set was an AUC of about 0.74.

How it works

  1. Data: around 1,000 patients with about 24,000 gene expressions each, plus a hidden test set.
  2. Pick candidate genes four ways: highest variance (then mRMR to cut redundancy), top limma hits, top robust limma hits, and genes reported in the literature (LYVE1, LILRB5, IMPDH2). Together: 105 genes.
  3. Rank them by random forest importance and try six cut-offs, from all 105 genes down to 12.
  4. Fit an elastic net (glmnet) for each cut-off, searching the mixing parameter alpha from 0 to 1 and scoring by cross-validated AUC.
  5. Check stability by repeating the best model with many random seeds.

What I found

The best cut-off kept 58 genes. On the training data its AUC was about 0.76. Across different random seeds it stayed between 0.75 and 0.77 (standard deviation 0.005), so the result does not depend on a lucky split. On the unseen test set it reached 0.733, close to the 0.74 target.

Caveat

The 0.76 figure comes from the training data, so it is optimistic; the 0.733 on unseen data is the number to trust. The original report calls AUC “accuracy”, which is not the same thing.
